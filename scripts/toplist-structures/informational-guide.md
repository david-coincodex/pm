## Structure: informational guide ("What is <X>?", "<X> explained")

The reader wants to understand something, not buy something yet. The page earns its place by
answering the question accurately and completely; the commercial payoff comes last and only
where it is genuinely relevant.

This is the highest factual-risk type in the set. Adult platforms have been through ownership
changes, verification purges, mass content removals and regional withdrawals, and a confidently
wrong sentence about any of that is worse than a vague one.

Build the article in this order:

1. **The direct answer first** (`<p>`, 1–2 paragraphs)
   - Answer the title question in the first two sentences, plainly. No preamble, no "in the
     world of adult entertainment".

2. **`<h2>How it works</h2>`** + 2–4 `<p>`
   - The mechanics: what you get, what you don't, how access works, what the limits are.
   - A `<ul>` of concrete inclusions where that is clearer than prose.

3. **2–4 further `<h2>` sections** covering the sub-questions a reader actually has
   - Choose these from the supplied context, not from a template. Typical: what it costs in
     general terms, how it compares to the free tier, what content is exclusive, regional
     availability, how to cancel.
   - Where a section names something in our catalog, a single `site-card` for that site is
     appropriate. At most one per article.

4. **`<h2>Is it worth it?</h2>`** + 1–2 `<p>`
   - An honest verdict including who should NOT bother. A guide that recommends everything to
     everyone is worthless and reads as advertising.

5. **The alternative** — only if we have something relevant
   - One `site-card-list` of up to 3 catalog sites that serve the same want, introduced as
     "if you want <the thing this doesn't do>". Skip entirely if nothing genuinely fits.

6. **FAQs** (in the `faqs` array, NOT in `content`)
   - 4–5 real questions, answered in 1–3 plain-text sentences each.

## Rules specific to this type

- **State only what the supplied sources establish.** If the context does not confirm a
  detail — current ownership, a policy, a date, what a tier includes — either omit it or write
  it as the general case without a specific claim. Do NOT reason from what you remember; this
  subject area changes and your recollection may be years out of date.
- **No prices, percentages or trial terms in prose.** Describe tiers qualitatively.
- **No dates or version numbers you cannot source.** "Since 2020" is a factual claim.
- Where something is genuinely contested or has changed repeatedly, say that it has changed
  rather than picking a version and asserting it.
- Never imply we operate, host, or are affiliated with the platform being explained.
